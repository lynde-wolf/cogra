### Thanks for visiting this docsite about CogRA (Cognitive Revolution Aesthetic)!
Here's a blurb about why I made this package in the first place

During my PhD I've read a great deal of literature especially from the
cognitive revolution, a period which started roughly in the 1950s and is still going to now.
Some of the foundational papers for my doctoral work as well as many others in my field were published in the pre-computer era. While
reading, I really appreciated the aesthetics that are due to (a) hand-drafted figures (b) specific typesetting requirements for the journals of
the time and (c) the archival scanning process. The archival process which involves scanning frequently introduces artifacts like noise, dust, and loss of those crisp edges. That gives it a worn look in the scanned image which I tried to recreate here.
The overall effect being something recognizable to me and my peers as specific to a timeframe of our field which is an interesting learned association.

While I doubt any figure generated with the CogRA package would actually get accepted to a journal, the look is fun and if I'm wrong about it being used for publications let me know. Also it was fun to dive into the history of graphical representation in statistics. I'll link a little bit of the work I leaned on for making this package. Enjoy!

### The articles that I used as exemplars for styling

Cohen, J. D., Dunbar, K., & McClelland, J. L. (1990). On the control of
automatic processes: A parallel distributed processing account of the Stroop
effect. *Psychological Review*, *97*(3), 332–361.
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.97.3.332>

Mayer, R. E. (1980). Elaboration techniques that increase the meaningfulness
of technical text: An experimental test of the learning strategy hypothesis.
*Journal of Educational Psychology*, *72*(6), 770–784.
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.72.6.770>
(The page layout of `cogra.paper` is measured from this article.)

Rumelhart, D. E., Hinton, G. E., & Williams, R. J. (1986). Learning
representations by back-propagating errors. *Nature*, *323*(6088), 533–536.
<https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0>
